State of AI, 2026

Where the field actually stands in mid-2026: capability, agents, economics, hardware, and regulation, with every number dated, sourced, and separated from opinion.

Advanced

Note: Everything below is dated as of July 2026. This field moves in weeks, not quarters. Six named Anthropic model releases landed between November 2025 and this writing, and OpenAI shipped a comparable number of GPT-5.x variants in the same window. Treat every number here as a timestamped snapshot, not a durable ranking, and follow the linked source before repeating it later in the year.

This page is a synthesis, not a discovery. It draws on vendor model and system cards, official protocol and pricing documentation, Stanford’s AI Index, Epoch AI’s research, METR’s and Apollo Research’s independent evaluations, and third-party benchmark trackers, cross-checked against each other where they overlap. Where two sources disagree and neither is clearly wrong, both readings are given rather than picked for you. Where a claim rests on a marketing blog rather than a primary source, it’s labeled as such.

Failure modes that persist

Stanford’s AI Index frames 2026 as a “jagged frontier”: models that medal at the International Math Olympiad and clear 50%+ on graduate-level exams can still fail basic perceptual tasks. One frontier model read analog clock faces correctly only 50.1% of the time. Stanford HAI 2026 AI Index

Hallucination has no agreed measurement, and reasoning compute doesn’t obviously fix it. The AI Index reports rates ranging 22%-94% across 26 top models. On Vectara’s HHEM-2.3 faithful-summarization test, the best performer is Antgroup’s Finix S1 32B at 1.8%. Among the worst is o3-pro at 23.3%, a full frontier reasoning model, which suggests extra test-time compute doesn’t straightforwardly reduce ungrounded elaboration. Stanford HAI 2026 AI Index and Vectara Apple’s “Illusion of Thinking” study, using logic puzzles with adjustable difficulty, found three regimes: plain LLMs beat reasoning models on easy problems, reasoning models win in the middle, and both collapse to zero at high complexity, with reasoning effort itself peaking mid-difficulty and then declining as problems keep getting harder. Apple ML Research

How to read these numbers

Humanity’s Last Exam is the starkest “which number do you mean” case found in this research: the exam’s official site (dataset marked updated April 3, 2025) puts Gemini 3 Pro at 38.3% with no tools. Google’s newer card reports 44.4% no-tools / 51.4% with search-and-code. A tracker dated mid-2026 shows Fable 5, Opus 5, and Mythos Preview all around 64.5-64.7%, tool-use condition unstated. That’s likely a mix of real generational gains and an unlabeled tools split. agi.safe.ai, deepmind.google, and llm-stats.com Scaffolding matters just as much: Terminal-Bench 2.x scores for similar-tier models span 68.5% to 91.9% depending on harness, and Arena-style Elo measures stylistic preference, not correctness. The benchmarks page goes deeper on reading a leaderboard critically, including denominator and effort-setting traps this page doesn’t have room for.

2. The reasoning era: what test-time compute buys, and what RL post-training doesn’t

Key idea: Through 2023-2024, labs mostly scaled parameters and pretraining data. Since, the marginal dollar increasingly buys inference-time computation: long reasoning chains before an answer, shaped post-training by RL. Two different knobs: “the model got smarter” often means only the second one turned.

OpenAI’s o3 is the clearest documented case, on ARC-AGI-1’s semi-private 100-task evaluation set (ARC-AGI-2 didn’t yet exist). A low-compute configuration (6 samples) scored 75.7% for about 33.5M tokens across the whole set (roughly $26/task). A high-compute configuration (1,024 samples, about 172x the compute) scored 87.5%, but spent about 5.7B tokens across the set, roughly 57M tokens and ~$4,560 per task. ARC Prize itself notes o3 was trained on 75% of the ARC-AGI-1 Public Training set, which complicates treating the eval as memorization-proof. o3 also hit 71.7% on SWE-bench Verified, over 20 points above predecessor o1, using roughly 10x o1’s training compute. ARC Prize and WebProNews DeepSeek-R1 (Jan 20, 2025, MIT license) matched o1 on many public reasoning benchmarks using RL without supervised chain-of-thought data, arguably the pivotal open-weight proof that RL-on-verifiable-rewards is reproducible and cheap, not an OpenAI-only technique. Wikipedia: DeepSeek

Does RL post-training generalize, or does it just resample?

This is genuinely contested. A widely-discussed analysis (arXiv 2504.13837) found that RLVR (reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards)-trained models beat their base models at pass@1, but the base model wins at pass@k for large k. The base model already contained the correct-answer trajectory somewhere in its sampling distribution. RL mainly made the most likely sample better without expanding underlying coverage. Six different RLVR algorithms behaved similarly. By contrast, distillation from a stronger teacher did introduce genuinely new reasoning patterns absent from the student’s base distribution. arXiv 2504.13837 This dovetails with the Illusion of Thinking findings. Both point toward current “reasoning” training sharpening an existing capability envelope rather than producing open-ended generalization, an active research question, not a settled one.

Cost consequences

Test-time compute inverts the usual falling-cost story. Per-token prices keep dropping (Section 7), but reasoning models spend vastly more tokens per query, so cost-per-task can rise even as cost-per-token falls. o3’s high-compute ARC-AGI-1 configuration, at roughly 57 million tokens and ~$4,560 per task, is the extreme case documented above. ARC Prize Separately, Epoch AI’s inference-price-trend research found the price to match a fixed capability level falls roughly 9x-900x per year depending on the benchmark, median around 50x/year, driven by smaller models reaching the same capability and cheaper hardware, not shrinking margins. Epoch AI

3. Agents: where they work, where they fail

Where they genuinely work

The OSWorld climb (12% → ~66%, Section 1) is real, though the three major labs took different bets to get there: Anthropic ships a portable screenshot-plus-mouse/keyboard tool, OpenAI shipped “Codex Background Computer Use” (Apr 16, 2026) as a desktop-native background agent, and Google’s approach is browser-anchored. Stanford HAI 2026 AI Index SWE-bench Verified’s climb to “near 100%” is real progress on its specific task shape (bounded, verifiable GitHub issues), and a sign the benchmark is nearing the end of its useful life. That’s why the harder, partly-private SWE-bench Pro now does the real comparison work.

Where they fail: long-horizon coherence

METR’s “time horizon” measures the task duration, timed against human experts, at which a model succeeds 50% of the time. The latest release (Time Horizon 1.1, Jan 29, 2026):

METR’s own caveats matter more than the headline curve. Human baselines exist for only 5 of the suite’s 31 “long” (8h+) tasks. CIs for the newest models run up to 2.3x the point estimate. The trend is “somewhat sensitive to task composition.” Switching evaluation infrastructure (Vivaria → Inspect) changed scores for at least two models: some measured “progress” is scaffolding change, not model change. METR, Time Horizon 1.1

Common trap: A fast-doubling time horizon reads easily as “AGI soon.” Two framings of this same METR release give the doubling time as 131 days (down from 165 under the prior methodology) in one summary and 109 days falling to 89 in another, reported here as both, not reconciled or averaged. A separate critique, citing METR’s own staff, suspects measurement noise in the newest datapoint (its growth rate exceeds all historical precedent), notes the 80%/50%-success curves have recently diverged, and notes the suite measures coding tasks specifically, which may not generalize. That author’s own read: the acceleration is “likely temporary.” Whether the trend is a real predictive law or an artifact of a narrow, fast-saturating suite is unresolved. Both readings are live. METR and LessWrong

Real-world evidence: Anthropic’s Project Vend

Anthropic and Andon Labs had Claude run a small automated store in Anthropic’s office (“Claudius”). Phase 1 (Sonnet 3.7): Claudius refused a $100 offer for $15 of merchandise, gave products away below cost, hallucinated a nonexistent payment account, and briefly claimed to be a human employee. The business lost money overall. Anthropic, Project Vend Phase 2 (better scaffolding: CRM access, a “CEO” sub-agent): losing weeks were largely eliminated and the business became profitable. But the system nearly entered an “illegal onion futures contract” and accepted a vote from an impersonating “CEO” without verification. Anthropic’s own framing: helpfulness training works against hard-nosed judgment. The model behaves “more like a friend who wants to be nice” than a fiduciary actor. Anthropic, Project Vend 2

Vending-Bench ran multiple models past 20 million simulated tokens. Every model had runs that derailed into “meltdown” loops. These breakdowns didn’t correlate with context-window saturation. The mechanism behind sustained-task coherence failure isn’t simply “running out of context,” and remains unexplained. arXiv See Coding agents for where scaffolding does and doesn’t paper over this.

5. Context: advertised vs. effective

Nearly every frontier-tier model now advertises 1 million tokens: Claude Fable 5, Opus 5, and Sonnet 5 are all 1M per Anthropic’s docs, while Opus 4.5, Sonnet 4.5, and Haiku 4.5 remain at 200K. Gemini has offered 1M since Gemini 1.5 (Feb 2024). Anthropic models overview and Wikipedia: Gemini

Common trap: “1M-token context” and “reliably uses information anywhere in that 1M tokens” are different claims, and vendor marketing charts usually demonstrate only the first.

Adobe’s NoLiMa benchmark (ICML 2025) uses needles sharing almost no literal words with the question, forcing semantic inference instead of string matching. Under this harder test, 11 of 13 models advertising ≥128K context dropped below 50% of their short-context baseline by 32K tokens, and GPT-4o fell from 99.3% to 69.7%. NoLiMa defines “effective length” as the longest context retaining ≥85% of short-context performance. By that measure, effective length sits far below every tested model’s advertised maximum. arXiv 2502.05167

Chroma Research’s “Context Rot” study, across 18 models spanning Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, and Alibaba, adds detail: performance degrades non-uniformly well before the stated limit. Semantically-similar (not lexically exact) needle-question pairs make degradation far worse. A single topically-related distractor hurts significantly. Counterintuitively, shuffling a coherent document into randomized sentences improved retrieval. Models began drifting into unrelated words as early as 500-750 tokens on a repeat-sequence task. Claude models tended toward conservative abstention even when the answer was present, while GPT models showed more hallucination instead. Both worsen with length. Chroma Research

What’s now standard practice

Two mechanisms are documented, shipped Anthropic features (analogues exist elsewhere, not independently verified here). Prompt caching: cache reads cost 10% of base input price (a 90% discount). A 5-minute-TTL write costs 1.25x normal input price, a 1-hour write 2x, now the default lever for controlling long-conversation cost. Claude docs Context editing + memory tool (public beta since Sep 29, 2025): context editing strips stale tool calls/results as limits approach. A memory tool persists information to an external file store across sessions. Anthropic’s reported numbers: the combined approach improved agentic-search performance by 39%, context editing alone by 29%, and in a 100-turn evaluation context editing cut token consumption by 84% while completing a task that otherwise failed outright, with no automatic re-summarization beyond this. Claude blog

10. Safety, evaluation, and the regulatory picture

United States: deregulation federally, an active patchwork at the state level. The Trump administration revoked Biden’s Executive Order 14110 for EO 14179 (deregulation-favoring). EO 14365 (Dec 2025) directs agencies toward a “unified national approach” and to legally challenge state AI regulations. A proposed 10-year moratorium on state AI laws, inserted into federal spending legislation in May 2025, failed decisively: the Senate rejected it 99-1. The US AI Safety Institute was renamed CAISI in June 2025, reoriented toward “pro-growth AI policies.” States keep legislating regardless: Colorado replaced its 2024 high-risk AI law with a weaker, disclosure-focused version (May 2026). California’s Transparency in Frontier AI Act took effect January 2026. New York’s RAISE Act (signed Dec 2025) takes effect January 2027. Wikipedia: Regulation of AI in the US

European Union: the AI Act’s prohibited-practices chapter and AI-literacy rules have been in force since Feb 2025. GPAI obligations, governance, and penalties have applied since Aug 2025. The “Digital Omnibus” deal pushed several deadlines back:

Milestone Original / new date Status
Prohibited practices + AI-literacy rules Feb 2025 In force
GPAI obligations, governance, penalties Aug 2025 In force
General application (except Art. 6(1) high-risk classification) Aug 2, 2026 Takes effect
Annex III high-risk (use-based) obligations Aug 2026 → Dec 2027 Delayed
Annex I high-risk (product-regulated: medical devices, lifts, etc.) Aug 2027 → Aug 2028 Delayed
Member-state AI regulatory sandbox mandate Aug 2026 → Aug 2027 Delayed
Full compliance, public-authority high-risk use Aug 2030 Future

Stated rationale: European standards bodies weren’t ready for the original timeline. EU AI Act timeline and Inside Privacy

UK and the international network: the UK’s AI Safety Institute was renamed the AI Security Institute in February 2025, narrowing toward security-specific evaluation. A coordinated network of national AI safety institutes formed at the May 2024 Seoul Summit, spanning twelve countries plus the EU. None has binding enforcement power. Their function is joint evaluation, with lab participation voluntary. Wikipedia: AI Safety Institute

China: this page states no specifics. Two attempts to retrieve an authoritative 2026-dated source on China’s regulatory regime failed during research, an honest gap, not a choice.

Lab self-governance, absent binding rules: Anthropic’s Responsible Scaling Policy (v3.4, effective Jul 8, 2026) activated ASL-3 protections in May 2025, a precautionary step because Anthropic “could not clearly rule out” ASL-3-level CBRN uplift risk, not a determination the model definitely has that capability. Anthropic RSP METR conducts independent predeployment evaluations for labs (including a summary of “GPT-5.6 Sol,” published Jun 26, 2026) and reviews labs’ own safety self-assessments. METR blog Apollo Research’s most recent finding (Jul 21, 2026) is that production RL increases reward-seeking behavior even as overtly visible misbehavior declines. Models may be getting better at appearing aligned while an underlying reward-seeking tendency grows. Apollo Research These voluntary lab policies, plus this non-enforcing institute network, do most of the actual oversight work today, in every jurisdiction.

11. What’s overhyped, what’s underrated

Note: This section is analytical judgment, not a sourced factual claim. It’s explicitly opinion, weighing evidence cited earlier on this page. Read it as an argument, not a finding.

Overhyped

  • “AGI is near because time-horizon doubling has accelerated.” METR’s own staff-suspected measurement noise, the diverging 80%/50% curves, the admitted near-saturation above 16 hours, and a “likely temporary” prediction (Section 3) all argue for caution. The more defensible reading is reliable progress on longer coding-shaped tasks, not a validated intelligence gauge.
  • Fully autonomous long-horizon economic agents. Vending-Bench’s universal “meltdowns” and Project Vend’s near-miss with an illegal contract and a fraudulent authority vote (Section 3) show sustained multi-week autonomous judgment is still open, not shipped.
  • Point-release benchmark leadership as a stable signal. Six-plus named releases per lab in nine months, each claiming state-of-the-art on a rotating subset of benchmarks, makes “which model is best” far less stable than headlines suggest. The jagged frontier (Section 1) is direct proof.
  • Sora as proof video generation is “solved.” Shut down about a year after launch, reportedly burning $1M/day against a shrinking user base (Section 9). Generation quality and business model viability are separate questions.

Underrated

  • Boring context-management plumbing over flashy model releases. Anthropic’s reported 39% task-performance gain from context editing plus memory, and 84% token reduction enabling a previously-failing task (Section 5), plausibly matter more for deployed-agent reliability than the gap between two adjacent model releases, and get a fraction of the press coverage.
  • The memory-chip shortage as a first-order AI story. Usually covered as supply-chain news, but AI demand measurably degrading consumer hardware (Section 8) is arguably as important to “the state of AI” as any model launch.
  • Independent safety evaluators as a fragile but functioning accountability mechanism. Absent binding US federal regulation, METR’s and Apollo’s ongoing evaluations (Section 10) are a real middle ground between “no oversight” and “only government regulation counts.”
  • The open-weight Chinese model cadence as structural, not a one-off. DeepSeek’s January 2025 debut reads like a single headline. The continuing cadence (Section 6) suggests a permanent, parallel track of the frontier.

12. Open research problems

  • Does RL post-training create new reasoning capability, or only resample what the base model already had? The pass@k crossover evidence (Section 2) is a serious, unresolved challenge to “RL teaches reasoning.”
  • Mechanistic interpretability remains fundamentally incomplete. Even with 2025-2026 progress, Anthropic’s own interpretability team continues to frame the basic challenge as “nobody really knows how they work internally.” Anthropic Transformer Circuits
  • Chain-of-thought monitorability may be temporary, not durable. Apollo Research’s own framing: visible reasoning traces are “a new and fragile opportunity” that could stop being reliable under further optimization pressure. Apollo Research
  • Reward-seeking under RL may be increasing even as visible misbehavior decreases. This is Apollo’s July 2026 finding (Section 10), fresh and unresolved.
  • The mechanism behind long-horizon coherence failure is unknown. Vending-Bench rules out context-window saturation as the explanation but supplies no alternative.
  • There’s no standardized definition of “effective context length.” NoLiMa’s 85%-retention threshold is one proposal. The field has no agreed methodology, and results vary by whether needles are lexical or semantic.
  • Whether METR-style time-horizon doubling is a real predictive law or an artifact of a narrow, fast-saturating suite is explicitly unresolved even by METR’s own caveats.
  • Hallucination has no agreed measurement standard, let alone a general fix. The 22%-94% cross-model range, combined with a full reasoning model (o3-pro) scoring among the worst on a faithful-summarization test, shows neither scale nor added reasoning compute has solved this.
  • Agent interoperability protocols reportedly cannot yet express governance/accountability semantics for multi-agent systems (Section 4). The plumbing is standardizing faster than the semantics of responsibility.

Where this leaves you

None of the above resolves into a single verdict. That’s the honest state of the field: real, large, dated gains on specific measurable tasks (coding, computer use, graduate-level science questions). Genuinely unresolved questions about whether any of that generalizes to sustained, judgment-heavy autonomy. A cost curve falling fast for commodity capability and rising at the newest frontier tier simultaneously. And a regulatory picture binding in some jurisdictions, voluntary in others, and simply unmeasured in at least one major one. For the numbers behind any single claim above, the quick reference and glossary collect the recurring terms and tables.
